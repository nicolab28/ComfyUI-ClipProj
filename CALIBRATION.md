# How the projection is built, and what it costs

Everything here was measured on the machine that produced the published matrices. Where a number is extrapolated from a shorter run, it says so.

## What is being fitted

```
cond = ((h - mean_in) / std_in) @ W * std_out + mean_out
```

`h` is the hidden state of the small encoder at one layer, `cond` is what the 32B would have produced at the same token position. The `-mlp` files add the output of a small residual network fed the same standardised input.

That is the whole model. There is no attention, no sequence mixing, no context: token `i` of the small model maps to token `i` of the large one, independently of its neighbours.

## Why a position-by-position map is possible

Every Qwen3-VL shares the same tokenizer, 151936 tokens. The same prompt produces the same ids at the same positions in a 4B, an 8B and the 32B, so the two sequences are already aligned and a pairing exists. This was checked rather than assumed: 472582 tokens compared across the family, no divergence.

That property is also the single point where the whole thing breaks most easily, see below.

## The corpus

Base prompts come from [GokuScraper/seedance-2-prompts-datasets](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GokuScraper/seedance-2-prompts-datasets), the English field only. They are short video descriptions, not H3 prompts, so they are expanded into the full H3 format (subject definitions, timecoded shots, soundscape) by Mistral and Gemini, using the system prompt in [`calibration/corpus_system_prompt.txt`](calibration/corpus_system_prompt.txt). Each seed goes through both engines, which costs nothing extra in fitting and gives two different phrasings of the same scene.

The `-celeb` matrices add the 500 most popular people on TMDB: five plain sentences and two full H3 prompts per person. That part is generated by [`calibration/corpus.py`](calibration/corpus.py).

| | prompts | training tokens | test tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| general corpus | 6797, of which 5664 fitted and 1133 held out | 1137358 | 219872 |
| with the 500 names | same split | 1530370 | 219872 |

The test prompts are held out whole, never by truncating a concatenated stream. Truncating after concatenation shifts every subsequent prompt by the residue of the previous one, and the misalignment accumulates down the file.

## Stage 1, encoding, which is where the time actually goes

Every prompt is passed through the 32B once and through the small model once, keeping the hidden state at the chosen layer. Tap 24 of 36 is used for both the 4B and the 8B, selected by a sweep over all layers.

Measured on one RTX 3090, two batch sizes so that the fixed cost separates from the per-prompt cost:

| | fixed cost | per prompt | per token |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-VL-32B, NVFP4, 15.7 GB | 143 s | 0.31 s | 1.37 ms |
| Qwen3-VL-4B, bf16 | 14 s | 0.15 s | not separated |

For the 6797-prompt corpus that extrapolates to roughly 35 minutes for the 32B pass and 17 minutes for the 4B, on a single card. [`calibration/run.py`](calibration/run.py) shards the corpus across two 3090s, so the whole encoding stage lands under an hour.

Output is stored in bf16, because the fit promotes to float32 for accumulation anyway and doubling the precision would only double the disk:

```
target32b_full.pt     13.0 GB    the 32B output
input4b_aligned.pt    19.5 GB    the 4B, three taps kept
input8b_aligned.pt    10.4 GB    the 8B, one tap
```

These are read back with `torch.load(..., mmap=True)`. A 10.4 GB file opens in about two seconds and costs 0.4 GB of RAM, which matters when three of them are open at once.

### The mistake worth knowing about

The first full-corpus calibration returned a test cosine of **0.0030**, which is nothing at all. The corpus was fine. The encoding script let each model tokenise the prompt on its own side, and the two chat templates do not segment identically: 225 tokens on average against 202, with the gap varying from 0 to 39 depending on the prompt. So the pairs drifted along the sentence and the regression was learning to predict one word from another.

The ids from the first encode are now imposed on the second (`H3_IDS`). After re-encoding, the length gap is zero on all 6797 prompts and both sides count exactly 1364027 tokens.

If you redo this on another model family, check that first. Nothing downstream will warn you.

## Stage 2a, the matrix

Ridge regression in closed form. One streaming pass over the corpus accumulating `XtX` and `XtY` in correlation form, then a solve. No gradients, no epochs, no learning rate, no seed, no early stopping. Running it twice gives the same matrix to the last digit.

`lambda` is swept over `1e1` to `1e6` and picked on the held-out set. 1000 wins on every configuration tried. Stronger regularisation measurably destroys named identities before it improves anything else.

Measured on one RTX 3090, reading the encodings from disk: **39 s** for a single lambda including load, and **38 s** for the full six-lambda sweep. The sweep is free, within noise of a single fit: the streaming accumulation is the entire cost, and each additional lambda is one solve of a 2560 x 2560 system plus an evaluation pass. Both runs reproduced the published `cos_test` of 0.7170 exactly, which is also a determinism check.

An important non-result: calibrating against the output of the DiT's `condition_proj` layer and mapping back through the pseudo-inverse looks like a large gain, 0.697 to 0.845, and changes nothing. Unregularised least squares is invariant to an invertible transform of the targets. The two matrices agree with each other at cosine 0.999998 where two genuinely different matrices give 0.744. See [MEASUREMENTS.md](MEASUREMENTS.md).

## Stage 2b, the residual network

The linear map is at its ceiling: eight times more calibration data buys 1.8 % of cosine, and eleven times buys 0.014. More prompts will not move it. A non-linear correction does.

```
d_in -> 16384 -> 5120,  GELU,  one hidden layer
126M parameters for the 4B,  151M for the 8B
```

The output layer is initialised at **zero**, so an untrained network reproduces the ridge exactly and training can only add to it. There is no risk of ending up below the matrix you started from, and the file is only written if it beats the ridge on the held-out set.

Trained for 120 epochs, batch 8192 for the 4B and 4096 for the 8B, lr 1e-3, weight decay 0.01, patience 8. Autocast to bf16 on the network only, the linear part staying in float32: that took a batch from 270 ms to 110 ms, a factor 2.45, and it was compute that was the bottleneck rather than data feeding.

Measured on one RTX 3090 for the 4B: 17 s to load the encodings, then **25 s per epoch**, so about **50 minutes** for the full 120. Most of the gain arrives long before the end, though. Two epochs already give 0.7489 against 0.7170 for the ridge alone, which is a third of the final improvement in under two percent of the time. If you are exploring rather than publishing, stop early.

Width beat depth at equal parameter count, which is worth knowing before spending a night on it:

| shape | test cosine |
|---|---|
| 16384 x 1 | 0.7944 |
| 8192 x 2 | 0.7691 |

The residual is stored and now loaded in fp16, 240 MB on the card for the 4B and 288 MB for the 8B. Node 0.1.4 or later is required, earlier versions cast it back up to float32.

## The attention sink

Token 0 of any sequence is an attention sink. Its direction is constant from one prompt to the next, cosine 1.0000 measured over 1966 prompts, it carries nothing from the text, and its norm reaches 16500 against 291 for a text token.

It is excluded from the fit, because its extreme values would wreck the statistics, and its measured value is substituted at inference. Since the vector is constant, that substitution is exact rather than approximate. Projecting it through a matrix that never saw one produces an arbitrary direction of enormous norm, invisible on a 200-token prompt where it is 0.5 % of the positions and ruinous on a 7-token one where it is 14 %.

## Results

All on the same held-out set, tap 24, lambda 1000.

| matrix | encoder | residual | test cosine |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mmh3-4b-ClipProj` | Qwen3-VL-4B | no | 0.7169 |
| `mmh3-4b-ClipProj-mlp` | Qwen3-VL-4B | yes | 0.7944 |
| `mmh3-4b-ClipProj-celeb` | Qwen3-VL-4B | no | 0.7095 |
| `mmh3-4b-ClipProj-celeb-mlp` | Qwen3-VL-4B | yes | 0.7930 |
| `mmh3-8b-ClipProj` | Qwen3-VL-8B | no | 0.7528 |
| `mmh3-8b-ClipProj-mlp` | Qwen3-VL-8B | yes | 0.7970 |
| `mmh3-8b-ClipProj-celeb` | Qwen3-VL-8B | no | 0.7466 |
| `mmh3-8b-ClipProj-celeb-mlp` | Qwen3-VL-8B | yes | 0.8037 |

The `-celeb` rows are slightly lower on the general corpus and that is the whole trade: adding the names costs 0.007 there and takes the reconstruction of name tokens from 0.8265 to 0.8844.

A cosine of 0.71 sounds poor and is not. The DiT tolerates far more than the metric suggests, and cosine does not rank the matrices in the order that generation does. Treat it as a fitting diagnostic, not a quality score.

## Hardware

Five consumer GPUs, no cloud, nothing rented:

```
2x RTX 3090          24 GB    encoding, the 32B needs the capacity
1x RTX 4070 Ti Super 16 GB    residual training and generation tests
1x RTX 4070          12 GB
1x RTX 3060          12 GB
```

End to end, from raw prompts to a published matrix, the whole thing is an evening on one machine:

| stage | one RTX 3090 |
|---|---|
| encoding with the 32B | ~35 min |
| encoding with the 4B | ~17 min |
| the matrix, six lambdas | 38 s |
| the residual, 120 epochs | ~50 min |

The encoding is sharded over the two 3090s, so in practice it is closer to half of what the table says. Most of the time is the 32B reading text, and none of it is training.

## Scripts

Everything lives in [`calibration/`](calibration/).

| script | what it does |
|---|---|
| `promptgen.py` | expands seed prompts into H3 format through Mistral and Gemini |
| `corpus.py` | builds the celebrity corpus from a name list |
| `encode.py` | encodes one shard on one card, writes hidden states to disk |
| `run.py` | drives the encoding across both 3090s |
| `merge.py` | reassembles the shards by index |
| `fit.py` | the ridge, with the lambda sweep and the held-out evaluation |
| `mlp.py` | trains the residual on top of an existing matrix |
| `add_sink.py` | measures the sink vector and writes it into a matrix |
| `make_controls.py` | generates the zero and identity control matrices |
| `to_safetensors.py` | converts a legacy `.pt` matrix, verifying each tensor |
| `tokens.py` | compares tokenizers across the Qwen3-VL family |
| `probe.py`, `names.py`, `knows.py`, `length.py`, `language.py` | the measurement scripts behind [MEASUREMENTS.md](MEASUREMENTS.md) |

They are driven by environment variables rather than arguments, which is documented at the top of each file.
